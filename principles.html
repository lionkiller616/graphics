Gestalt Principles

Psychological Basis

Gestalt psychology emphasizes that humans naturally perceive patterns and wholes rather than disconnected parts. Our brains automatically organize visual elements into coherent groups based on principles like similarity, continuity, and closure.

🧠

The brain completes incomplete shapes (closure) to reduce cognitive load and make sense of visual information more efficiently.

Contrast

Psychological Basis

Contrast creates visual interest and directs attention. The human eye is drawn to areas of high contrast, making it a powerful tool for emphasizing important elements and creating visual hierarchy.

👁️

High-contrast elements are processed 20-30% faster by the visual cortex, making them essential for quick comprehension.

Balance

Psychological Basis

Balance creates visual stability and harmony. Humans have an innate preference for balanced compositions, which feel more comfortable and aesthetically pleasing. Symmetrical balance conveys formality, while asymmetrical balance creates dynamic interest.

⚖️

Studies show balanced designs are perceived as more trustworthy, while unbalanced compositions can create tension or excitement.

Visual Hierarchy

Psychological Basis

Visual hierarchy guides the viewer's eye through content in order of importance. The brain processes visual information sequentially, and hierarchy leverages this by creating clear focal points and flow patterns.

🔍

Users typically scan web pages in an F-shaped pattern, making top-left placement most valuable for important elements.

1. Primary
2. Secondary
3. Tertiary

Alignment

Psychological Basis

Alignment creates order and organization, reducing cognitive load. The brain seeks patterns and structure, and proper alignment satisfies this need while creating invisible connections between elements.

📐

Proper alignment can improve reading speed by up to 15% and comprehension by 20% by creating clear visual paths.

Repetition

Psychological Basis

Repetition creates rhythm and unity. The brain recognizes and remembers repeated patterns more easily, creating a sense of cohesion and brand recognition. It also establishes expectations that make interfaces more intuitive.

🔄

Repeating visual elements increases familiarity, which can improve user comfort and trust by up to 40%.

Proximity

Psychological Basis

Proximity creates relationships between elements. Objects placed close together are perceived as related, while space separates unrelated items. This principle helps organize information and reduce visual clutter.

👥

The brain groups elements within 50px as related, making proximity a powerful tool for information architecture.

Color Theory

Psychological Basis

Color evokes emotional responses and influences perception. Different wavelengths of light stimulate the brain in distinct ways, triggering emotional and physiological responses that vary by culture, context, and personal experience.

🌈

Color can affect mood, appetite, and even heart rate. For example, blue can lower blood pressure while red can increase it.

Red: Energy, passion, danger (increases heart rate)

Blue: Trust, calm, professionalism (most universally liked)

Green: Growth, health, environment (easiest on the eyes)

Yellow: Happiness, optimism, caution (most visible color)
