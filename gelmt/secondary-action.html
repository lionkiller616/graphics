Secondary Action

Adding Life to Animation

What is Secondary Action?

Secondary actions are subtle movements that complement and enrich the main action of a character or object. They add layers of realism, personality, and depth, making the animation feel more complex and believable.

These actions should support, not distract from, the primary focus. Examples include a character's facial expression while they are speaking, the sway of clothing as someone walks, or the jiggle of keys in a pocket. They provide visual cues that enhance the audience's understanding of the main action or the character's state.

Psychology Behind Secondary Action

Secondary actions are psychologically effective because they mirror the complex and layered movements we observe in the real world. Nothing moves in perfect isolation; there are always subtle reactions, shifts in weight, or accompanying environmental effects.
