Timing

The Soul of Animation

What is Timing?

Timing is arguably the most crucial principle in animation. It refers to the duration of an action – how many frames it takes for something to happen. Timing dictates the speed and rhythm of movement and is fundamental to conveying weight, physical properties, personality, and emotion.

A fast action might indicate something light or energetic, while a slow action suggests weight or reluctance. Varying the timing can change the entire mood and impact of a scene, making it feel comedic, dramatic, heavy, or light.

Psychology Behind Timing

The timing of a movement has a direct psychological impact because it taps into our real-world understanding of physics and emotion. Our brains are wired to associate certain timing patterns with specific physical properties and emotional states.

psychology_alt

Physical Perception

Fast movements feel light, slow movements feel heavy based on our real-world experiences with objects and physics.

mood

Emotional Impact

Quick timing creates excitement or urgency, while slow timing builds anticipation or seriousness.

person

Character Traits

Timing reveals personality - energetic characters move quickly, while thoughtful ones move deliberately.
