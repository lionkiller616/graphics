Anticipation

The Principle That Prepares Your Audience for Action

What is Anticipation?

Anticipation is a small preparatory movement that occurs before a major action. Its purpose is to telegraph to the audience that something is about to happen, making the main action more impactful and easier to follow.

Often, the anticipation movement is in the opposite direction of the main action. For example, a character might crouch down (anticipation) before jumping up (main action), or pull their arm back (anticipation) before throwing a punch (main action). It's a crucial element for adding realism and power to animated movements.

The Psychology Behind Anticipation

Anticipation works because it mirrors our everyday experience. In the real world, physical actions require preparation - a baseball pitcher winds up, a dancer prepares to leap, even blinking involves a slight closing before opening. Our brains are hardwired to recognize these preparatory cues.
