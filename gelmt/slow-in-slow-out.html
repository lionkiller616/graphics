Slow In & Slow Out

The Physics of Movement

What is Slow In & Slow Out?

Slow In and Slow Out, also known as "easing," is the principle that objects need time to accelerate at the beginning of a movement and decelerate at the end. In traditional animation terms, this meant drawing more frames near the start and end poses and fewer frames in the middle.

This principle adds realism to animation because it mimics how inertia and acceleration/deceleration work in the real world. Without slow in and slow out, motion appears mechanical and unnatural, as if the object instantly changes speed.

Psychology Behind Slow In & Slow Out

Slow In and Slow Out aligns animation with our fundamental understanding of physics. We intuitively expect objects with mass to take time to get up to speed and slow down, due to inertia and forces like friction.

physics

Inertia

Our brains expect objects to resist changes in motion, requiring time to accelerate or decelerate.

psychology

Believability

Eased motion matches our real-world experience, making animation feel more authentic.

visibility

Visual Comfort

Gradual changes are easier on the eyes and create more pleasing motion.
